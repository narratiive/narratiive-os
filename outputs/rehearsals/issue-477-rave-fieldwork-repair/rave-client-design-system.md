# Rave example client design system

Status: internal prototype for FIELDWORK and SKETCHBOOK. This is not an approved Rave Coffee identity and does not replace Rave's own brand system.

## Design idea

**A clear path through serious coffee.**

The shared system should feel knowledgeable without becoming solemn. A warm editorial base carries the evidence; saturated yellow and cobalt mark movement, decisions and creative energy. The client world stays recognisable across FIELDWORK and SKETCHBOOK while each product keeps a different job.

## Shared visual identity

### Colour

- Ink `#17130F`: primary type and high-contrast grounds.
- Paper `#F5EFE3`: primary FIELDWORK canvas and neutral SKETCHBOOK support.
- Signal Yellow `#FFCA05`: decisions, progression and directional emphasis.
- Cobalt `#1738A3`: evidence, references and analytical structure.
- Roast `#6B351F`: photographic bridge and tactile secondary accent.
- White `#FFFFFF`: reverse type and breathing space.

Yellow and cobalt should never compete at equal scale. Yellow carries direction; cobalt carries evidence.

### Typography

- Prototype family: Arial, selected for portability and reliable rendering.
- Display: bold, 42–64 pt, short and decisive.
- Section heading: bold, 28–36 pt.
- Body: regular, 18–24 pt.
- Evidence and captions: 10–12 pt, cobalt or neutral grey.
- Use sentence case. Avoid ornamental type, dense all-caps paragraphs and consulting-style label stacks.

### Photography

- Tactile, close and appetising: beans, grounds, paper, ceramic, hands and real preparation surfaces.
- Natural directional light with crisp shadow and visible texture.
- Everyday confidence rather than luxury ritual or barista theatre.
- Leave genuine negative space for the argument.
- Never use generic boardroom imagery, anonymous lifestyle stock or false product packaging.

The prototype uses one AI-generated internal editorial still life. It is not a Rave-owned asset and cannot be released externally without approval.

### Visual metaphor

The **yellow path** is the primary memory device. In FIELDWORK it shows strategic progression, sequencing and choices. In SKETCHBOOK it becomes a more expressive route through imagery, campaign moments and asset families.

Supporting metaphors:

- scattered beans becoming an ordered route;
- anchor, exploration and frontier as levels of customer confidence;
- evidence in cobalt, decisions in yellow.

### Layout

- 16:9 landscape, 64 px outer margin, twelve-column mental grid.
- One argument per page.
- One dominant visual or diagram, never a dashboard of cards.
- Page title at top left; product, client and status in a quiet footer.
- Evidence notes remain visible but subordinate.
- Use asymmetry and negative space; avoid repeated boxed modules.

### Asset presentation

- Every asset carries status: verified client, verified external, modelled, assumption or creative illustration.
- Show one hero asset at useful scale before contact sheets or variants.
- Preserve source identifiers, rights status, version and approval state backstage.
- Never imply that an AI-generated prototype is client-owned, approved or production-ready.

## Product expression

### FIELDWORK

Purpose: make the commercial argument legible.

- Paper and ink dominate.
- Cobalt signals evidence and open inputs.
- Yellow marks the decision or route through the argument.
- Photography appears selectively and never substitutes for evidence.
- Charts and models remain editable, plainly labelled and free of invented precision.

### SKETCHBOOK

Purpose: make the strategic direction tangible.

- Photography may become full-bleed or tightly cropped.
- Yellow becomes more kinetic; Roast may add warmth and tactility.
- Copy becomes shorter and more evocative, while status labels keep hypotheses honest.
- Territory pages, image treatments and asset examples use the same grid, type hierarchy and footer as FIELDWORK.

## Continuity test

A FIELDWORK page and SKETCHBOOK page should still feel related when their product labels are hidden. They should share palette, type, path motif, crop behaviour and editorial restraint. They should not be identical: FIELDWORK explains the choice; SKETCHBOOK shows what that choice could become.
