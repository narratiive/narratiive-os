# LOA creative brief — internal fictional rehearsal

Status: **creative illustration; Matt approval required before external production**

## 1. Business task

Prove whether one specific social occasion can turn LOA’s distinctive bottle into qualified trial and profitable repeat. This is not a national-awareness commission.

## 2. Audience hypothesis

UK adults who want a grown-up soft option in everyday social pauses. This audience definition is an assumption until founder and customer research is authorised.

## 3. Behaviour to change

Move from “that bottle looks interesting” to “LOA belongs in this occasion,” then from first taste to an observable second purchase.

## 4. Single-minded proposition

**A brighter pour for the social pause.**

## 5. Reason to believe

The bottle, coral colour and serve can form a repeatable mnemonic. Product taste, formulation, availability, demand and repeat are not verified and must not be claimed.

## 6. Creative territory

**A Brighter Pour** — ordinary, low-energy moments become vivid through the product ritual: bottle, opening, glass, bubbles, food and shared serve.

## 7. Distinctive assets

Coral/pink glass bottle, gold cap, cream LOA lettering, tactile condensation, bright natural serve. Reuse Paul’s existing internal LOA assets subject to provenance, rights and product-accuracy approval.

## 8. Tone

Warm, appetising, optimistic and adult. Never medicinal, worthy, hyperactive or built around unverified wellness claims.

## 9. Deliverables for the proof phase

- One proposition landing-page route.
- Three six-second product/occasion films.
- Six stills spanning food pairing, cooler discovery and shared serve.
- One sampling capture route and one replenishment CRM sequence.
- Retail sell-through and DTC repeat measurement plan.

## 10. Mandatory guardrails

No health, alcohol-replacement, stockist, formulation, sustainability or performance claims without evidence and legal review. No real people or places may be represented as LOA customers or stockists. No publication, spend, sampling or production booking without Matt’s exact approval.

## 11. Success and stop rules

Success requires brand attribution, occasion comprehension, qualified trial, retailer sell-through, second purchase and contribution to beat pre-agreed baselines. Stop if the territory is remembered but not attributed, trial does not convert to repeat, contribution is negative, stock/waste risk is unacceptable, or the claims cannot be substantiated.

## Unresolved inputs

Founder interview, product truth, authorised customer/category research, pack and formulation accuracy, usage rights, distribution availability, unit economics, budget, timing, legal review and named owners.
