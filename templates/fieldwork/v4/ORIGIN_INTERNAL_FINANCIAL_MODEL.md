**Sell the confidence behind the coffee.**

Prioritise established independent cafés and selected boutique hotel groups considering a change of supplier, equipment refresh or team-development investment. ## Bounded competitor audit and strategic judgement
Reviewed official wholesale/FAQ service descriptions for Union, Extract and Clifton, as of access date. All three publicly promote overlapping support capabilities. This is not an exhaustive competitor or advertising audit and does not establish relative quality, response times, market ownership or customer perceptions. We cannot claim Origin is uniquely full-service, that competitors neglect operating outcomes, or that Origin currently markets only coffee quality.

The specific marketing hypothesis: **established quality-conscious operators may be persuaded to switch when the practical value and transition credibility of Origin's expertise is made more compelling than an undifferentiated service list.** “Established” is a proposed segment, not a verified Origin growth priority. Test supplier-review timing, switching costs, consistency needs, staff capability, decision makers, service geography and willingness to pay. Secure permission and evidence before making performance or savings claims. If switching barriers outweigh value or competitors already own the position convincingly, change the territory.

## One account-acquisition model
The user has explicitly authorised an illustrative assumed-data scenario for this fictional mock-up. It is not a genuine Origin forecast or probability-weighted expected result. This supersedes v2's no-numbers treatment for the TEST example only. Never reuse the illustration as verified data in a live proposal.

**Incremental year-one revenue = incremental won accounts × assumed net annual revenue per account × year-one trading ramp factor.**

Horizon: first 12 months from a hypothetical acquisition programme launch after Fieldwork and implementation approval. The ramp captures staggered wins, onboarding, order build-up and retention during that window. “Won accounts” means incremental accounts that become trading customers beyond business as usual, not qualified leads. In this illustration one account equals one trading location; do not treat a hotel group as one account at the same economics without revising the definition.

VAT is excluded from revenue. Cost of goods, fulfilment, training, servicing, customer acquisition and implementation costs are NOT deducted: all results are revenue, not gross/contribution profit. Margins remain unknown and unmodelled. The year-one ramp must not be applied again if net account revenue already reflects the same partial-year cohort effects.

Sensitivity changes the count of additional trading accounts and timing/ramp, not an arbitrary percentage of total company revenue. These are deliberately illustrative variations, not empirically bounded intervals or probabilities. They hold assumed account economics fixed and omit other downside risks. Lower ramp can represent later wins/slower order build-up; higher ramp assumes earlier wins/faster ramp, requiring capacity and independent validation. Range is a sensitivity envelope, not a confidence interval.

At central count/ramp, changing assumed weekly volume to 10kg produces £59,400; 30kg produces £178,200. Holding 20kg/week and using £18/kg produces £97,200; £26/kg produces £140,400. These additional one-way checks expose dependence on unverified economics; they are internal only. A ±£1,000 change in annual account value changes the central illustration by ±£5,400.

No lead-to-win conversion is assumed. No percentage of 12,313 or 13,211 outlets is taken as achievable demand. Even 12 wins requires a genuinely reachable, qualified population and competitive offer; external market totals do not prove feasibility. No causal attribution, achieved ROI, payback, profit, client budget or guaranteed result is asserted. Fieldwork identifies and prioritises opportunities; subsequent implementation requires separate investment and may not deliver the illustrated revenue.
