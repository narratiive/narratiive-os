# Narratiive two-page commercial proposal design specification
Reference standard · v3 content/layout approved, v4 image refinement · reconstructed from the original Loom Loft spread · Matt owns final design

## Purpose and non-negotiable format
A premium commercial invitation to commission Fieldwork after successful discovery. Exactly two portrait pages, readable separately and as a spread. It is not the discovery invitation and not the Fieldwork strategy presentation. Original spread controls composition; this document formalises it, with evidence and terminology corrections.

## Art direction
Quiet editorial confidence. Warm off-white paper, near-black ink, tactile naturally lit photography, deep forest-green opportunity panel and sparing bronze rules/numerals. Large serif headlines carry the argument; clean sans text makes the commercial details easy to read. No dashboards, gradients, generic icons or decorative growth charts. Photography should feel observed, with craft, texture, people or product in context. It must be relevant and licensed; AI illustration must be labelled and never passed off as client-owned photography.

## Production tokens
The raster spread does not establish exact original fonts or colour values. The following are explicit reproducible candidate tokens, subject to Matt's visual approval, not recovered brand canon.
- Canvas: A4 portrait, 210 × 297 mm (595.28 × 841.89 pt); two individual pages; no printed spread gutter or drop shadow.
- Outer margin: 32 pt; 12 pt column gutters; three equal columns; fine rules 0.4 pt.
- Paper #F4F0E7; ink #131C18; forest #19372D; bronze #A66B27; muted #60655D; rule #C4C5BA.
- Headline: high-contrast editorial serif, candidate Baskerville regular; hero 42–48 pt, leading 43–49 pt. Secondary 30–34/34–37; section titles 16–19/20. Use an approved licensed original font if recovered; reflow and review after substitution.
- Body: candidate Arial regular, 9.5–10.5 pt / 13–14.5 pt. Short deck 12–13/16. Labels 7–8 pt with 1.3–1.8 pt tracking. Sources/status never below 7 pt. No body text baked into images.
- Hierarchy: Narratiive masthead, strategy / creative / growth strapline; client name; product label; page number and consistent footer. Real logo master replaces live-text masthead when supplied.

## Page 1 — the commercial reason
Top masthead and hairline. Client label and large, specific headline on the left; warm hero photograph on the right/behind, with a calm light area protecting text. A one-sentence standfirst connects a distinctive observation to the decision Fieldwork will resolve.

At roughly the middle of the page, place two adjacent panels: forest-green opportunity argument (left), paper commercial evidence/model (right). Do not make the opportunity panel a generic promise. The commercial panel either shows a defensible, sourced result with timeframe/basis or clearly names the inputs needed to establish value. Never insert default uplift percentages merely to fill the design.

Bottom band: three numbered facets of the selected opportunity (finding, tension and test), rather than three independent growth mechanisms. Use the same quiet editorial rhythm as the reference. These are observations or questions, each with a precise heading and short implication. Footer: agency line, version/status and 01/02. Aim for 230–290 words including notes; cut repetition before reducing type.

## Page 2 — the engagement and decision
Small client/product header and top rule. Large serif engagement headline, concise explanation and a narrow editorial pull-line (Narratiive-authored, never falsely attributed to the client).

Three image-led columns: opportunity map; strategic brand and go-to-market direction; practical activation roadmap. Each explains the decision enabled, rather than naming abstract outputs alone. Use distinct images for audience, value proposition and activation, chosen as one coherent editorial set. Different crops of one source photograph do not count as image variety.

Full-width photographic closing band with a short, legible editorial line. Below: engagement overview with seven-working-day commitment, approved investment and clear next step. A short prerequisites line connects the promise to its start condition. Keep exclusions readable; fuller approved terms can accompany the proposal. Footer 02/02.

## Content and exception behaviour
Use Fieldwork throughout. Remove legacy product labels from all client-facing text, metadata and filenames. Never fabricate a testimonial to populate the right-hand pull-line. For internal examples, visible fictional/internal status on both pages takes priority over decorative copy. Incomplete fee, discovery or commercial evidence blocks client release, not internal design review. Preserve the commercial panel even when there is no number; use a clearly designed evidence request rather than an empty chart.

## Image and export checks
Keep source images separately, record provenance/rights and crop coordinates, retain editable text and layout. Raster reference is a visual benchmark, not a full-page background to overwrite. Export exactly two pages with embedded fonts; check at 100% and as a spread for overflow, awkward wraps, colour contrast, image resolution, hierarchy and calm spacing. Extract text to confirm names, evidence labels, terms and no legacy terminology. Matt approves the exact final PDF version after design and commercial review. The included PDF is an internal candidate only.

## Version 2 content binding
Page 1 must reflect actual discovery (or state its absence in a fictional example), establish a non-obvious finding and tension, and develop ONE primary opportunity. Where supported, the evidence panel gives one dominant central result with discreet conservative/upside sensitivity, changed assumptions and confidence. Otherwise show the exact operating logic and missing inputs without invented numbers. Page 2 outputs are tailored to that decision; the fixed fee appears only after Matt's exact approval. The supplied Loom Loft spread remains the approved visual language; this reconstructed font/layout implementation is still Matt's review candidate.

## Permanent image-selection rule — reference standard
Each proposal requires a deliberate, varied selection relevant to the client and the argument. Use a distinct source image for each placement: hero, growth audience, value proposition, route-to-market/activation and closing context when present. Do not repeat the same photograph, generated scene or near-duplicate crop across those placements. Cohesion comes from shared art direction, palette, light and treatment, not repetition of one scene.

Define each slot's narrative purpose before selecting imagery. Vary subject, scale and context: human relationship for hero; operating environment for audience; practical expertise/detail for value; partnership or communication setting for activation; relevant service context for closing. Do not add unsupported logos, invented client facilities, people or product claims. Label generated/concept art appropriately and verify permissions/rights for external use. Record source asset, prompt or licence, crop, slot and approval status in the asset register. Review the whole two-page spread for relevance, distinctness and harmony before export. Preserve the approved typography, frame geometry, whitespace, colours and image-box dimensions.
