# Narratiive — Commercial Proposal Intelligence & Financial Modelling Standard

Implement this as the reusable commercial specification for all post-discovery Fieldwork proposals.

The objective is to create a persuasive, premium two-page proposal that demonstrates exceptional due diligence, identifies a commercially meaningful growth hypothesis, and makes commissioning Fieldwork feel like a considered, high-value investment.

Preserve the approved Loom Loft editorial visual language. Improve the strategic and commercial substance substantially.

## A. Mandatory strategic intelligence

Before drafting, synthesise Groundwork research with the discovery-call record.

Identify:

1. **The non-obvious finding:** A specific, evidenced observation about the client, its audience, market or competitive position.
2. **The strategic tension:** A contradiction, missed connection, unmet customer need or underexploited strength that could be holding growth back.
3. **The commercial mechanism:** How resolving that tension could change measurable behaviour and generate economic value.
4. **The evidence:** Source references, client-confirmed facts, assumptions and unresolved questions.
5. **The Fieldwork proposition:** The specific decisions Narratiive will help the client make, and what they will receive within seven working days.

Reject generic findings such as "the brand could reach new audiences" or "there is potential to improve differentiation" unless they are supported by a specific insight.

The proposal should contain at least one finding that a knowledgeable founder might genuinely find surprising or worth investigating.

Do not invent a surprising claim merely to create a wow moment. If the evidence does not support one, flag the research gap.

## B. Financial opportunity model

Model ONE primary growth opportunity per proposal.

Select the most commercially relevant mechanism supported by the evidence:

- E-commerce conversion: eligible traffic × conversion-rate change × average order value.
- Retention: eligible customers × incremental retained purchase frequency × net revenue per purchase.
- Subscription: subscriber cohort × change in retained paid periods × net subscription revenue, adjusting for churn and cohort overlap.
- Average order value: expected eligible transactions × incremental order value.
- Wholesale: qualified incremental partners × expected annual net revenue per partner, adjusted for conversion, onboarding and trading ramp-up.
- Other mechanisms: define the actual business-specific formula and assumptions.

Never default to a generic percentage uplift against total company revenue if a more specific operational model is available.

**Evidence hierarchy:**

1. Client-confirmed operating and financial data.
2. Verifiable company disclosures.
3. Relevant external benchmarks, with sources and limitations.
4. Explicitly labelled assumptions requiring validation.

Do not convert an assumption into a fact.

If the model lacks a defensible denominator, key operating driver or baseline, do not publish a financial forecast. Instead, present the specific commercial opportunity Fieldwork will quantify.

## C. Scenario rules

Show:

- ONE central, evidence-based scenario as the primary visual.
- A concise conservative/upside sensitivity around the central case.
- The assumptions that change between cases.
- The confidence level and evidence gaps.

The sensitivity must reflect plausible variation in the actual business driver, not predetermined 5%/10%/20% increments.

Use commercially relevant precision rather than false accuracy. Round revenue estimates appropriately to the size of the opportunity.

Distinguish additional revenue from incremental gross or contribution profit. Include implementation costs, margin assumptions, cannibalisation, timing and attribution limitations where they materially affect the interpretation.

Do not present revenue potential as a guaranteed return from commissioning Fieldwork.

Fieldwork identifies and prioritises the opportunity. Subsequent implementation may require separate investment.

## D. Bespoke Fieldwork pricing

Recommend one exact Fieldwork fee using:

- Verified annual revenue as commercial context.
- Number of markets, audience groups and business models.
- Research and analytical complexity.
- Agreed scope and tangible deliverables.
- Estimated specialist person-days and internal delivery cost.
- External costs and project risks.
- Required project profitability.

Use a provisional 55% target gross margin for internal estimation, not as a universal pricing mandate.

Fee floor = estimated direct delivery cost / (1 − target gross margin).

Adjust for justified scope or complexity differences. Do not price automatically as a fixed percentage of company turnover or the estimated upside.

Provide Matt with an internal pricing rationale and any material uncertainty. Matt approves the exact final quote.

The client-facing document displays only the approved fixed fee, clear scope, delivery terms and any relevant exclusions.

Do not invent client revenue, budget or approved commercial terms.

## E. Delivery promise

Fieldwork is delivered within seven working days from receipt of agreed inputs and completion of prerequisites.

Define the required inputs, included outputs, review process and any scope exclusions.

Do not call the engagement Growth Sprint or Blueprint Lite.

Use clear language that founders and business leaders understand.

## F. Two-page proposal structure

**Page 1 — The opportunity**

- A highly distinctive, client-specific headline.
- A concise reflection of what we heard in discovery.
- The non-obvious strategic finding.
- The commercial tension and why it matters.
- One visually compelling financial opportunity model, where evidence permits.
- A clear statement of what we would investigate through Fieldwork.

**Page 2 — The investment**

- A strong headline linking the opportunity to the proposed work.
- Three specific Fieldwork outputs tailored to the client, not generic agency deliverables.
- Why these outputs will help the client make better commercial decisions.
- Seven-working-day delivery commitment.
- One exact approved Fieldwork fee.
- Clear next step and approval mechanism.

Preserve premium editorial art direction, photography, restrained colour, excellent typography and strong visual hierarchy from the Loom Loft reference.

Avoid turning the proposal into a dense financial report. Keep detailed workings and evidence in the internal production brief, with an appropriate assumptions summary in the client document.

## H. Acceptance criteria

Reject the proposal if:

- The insight could apply equally to almost any brand.
- The financial scenario is derived from an arbitrary percentage.
- The fee is a generic price band instead of a specific approved quote.
- The document implies Fieldwork itself guarantees revenue growth.
- Key facts or financial assumptions lack provenance.
- The visual design fails to meet the Loom Loft reference quality.
- The copy contains legacy product terminology or unsubstantiated claims.

Do not invent Origin's financial results or discovery statements. Where information is unavailable, identify the precise missing inputs and demonstrate the financial logic internally without passing fictional numbers off as genuine.

Present the revised two-page proposal and internal financial model for Matt's review. Do not send externally.
