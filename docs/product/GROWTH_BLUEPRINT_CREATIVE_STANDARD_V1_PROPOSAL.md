# Creative standard — opening five slides
Status: design exploration; fictional FIELDWORK example. 2026-10-13.

## Design ambition
Investment-grade commercial argument meets world-class agency craft. One decisive idea per slide; each chart earns its place. Strong editorial typography, disciplined whitespace, restrained palette, purposeful contrast, no generic icons or ornamental data art. Source/assumption labels visible at point of claim. Slides must be editable and usable at presentation distance.

## Five-slide design directions
1. **The destination / The number:** Full-bleed dark editorial cover, oversized £690k and one-line 12-month opportunity. Baseline vs scenario labelled; strong note: hypothetical and unvalidated. The hook is not a guaranteed promise.
2. **The bridge / The mechanism:** Three-step waterfall from £1.20m to £1.89m: +£300k traffic, +£300k conversion, +£90k AOV. Footnote sequential decomposition and scenario assumptions.
3. **The constraint / The tension:** Three evidence hypotheses — qualified discovery, reason to choose, purchase friction — displayed as falsifiable decision tree. Highlight provisional diagnosis, not invented finding.
4. **The moves / The choices:** Three columns, each with decision, economic lever, validation experiment, trade-off and owner. Avoid claiming uplift before testing.
5. **The creative future / The proof of possibility:** Distinctive brand expression for fictional FIELDWORK, 'Built for the work of living', treated as a creative hypothesis. Founder-authentic, cinematic workplace-realism rather than stock lifestyle. Include what audience evidence must validate before selection.

## Slide QA
Headline <= 20 words ideally; chart titles state insight not metric; every numeric claim tagged as verified or modelled; min readable notes; no unexplained acronyms; colour is never the only encoding; no visuals suggesting fictional examples are real client work.

## Source-to-visual contract
Each visual declares visual_id, underlying calculation/claim ids, chart type, dimensions, editable source and source caption. Creative imagery requires provenance, rights and explicit illustration labelling.
