# Narratiive Marketing Ambition

**Status:** Canonical strategic reference  
**Date:** August 2026

## The ambition

Narratiive exists to help ambitious businesses find a clearer, more effective route to growth and then make that strategy tangible in market.

The long-term ambition is to be able to enter a business, understand its commercial objective, diagnose the barriers to growth, and build a credible 12-month plan capable of materially changing sales, customers, market share or margin.

A statement such as “we will double sales in 12 months” is not yet an external promise. Narratiive does not control product, pricing, distribution, competitive response, economic conditions, sales execution or a client’s willingness to implement recommendations. For now, doubling growth is an internal ambition: build the evidence, methodology and client results that may eventually justify stronger outcome claims.

## The proposition

**Narratiive finds the clearest route to growth for an ambitious business — then shows what it looks like in market.**

Clients do not primarily buy a strategy document. They buy answers to commercial questions:

- How do we grow faster?
- Why are more people not choosing us?
- Where can the next tranche of revenue plausibly come from?
- Who should we target?
- What should we become known for?
- What should we say?
- What marketing should we stop wasting money on?
- What should we make and where should it appear?

The Growth Blueprint is the intelligence architecture that answers those questions. Creative is the visible manifestation of the answer.

**Growth is the promise.  
Strategy is the architecture.  
Creative makes the strategy tangible.  
Tony makes delivery scalable.**

## The commercial product: Growth Sprint

The engagement is the **Narratiive Growth Sprint**. The intellectual product inside it is the **Growth Blueprint**.

Indicative duration: **2–3 weeks**.

A client may arrive with problems such as stalled growth, fragmented marketing, low awareness, unclear positioning, inefficient spend or uncertainty about what to say and make.

The Sprint produces:

1. Growth diagnosis
2. Commercial growth opportunity
3. Strategic positioning
4. Audience strategy
5. Narrative platform
6. Campaign platform
7. 90-day action plan
8. Prototype creative that demonstrates the strategy in market

The final presentation should not feel like receiving a strategy deck. It should feel like: **“This is what your company could become.”**

## Commercial ambition before marketing strategy

Every Blueprint should increasingly begin with a commercial equation rather than a marketing objective:

**Commercial ambition → growth equation → barriers → strategic interventions → creative expression → activation → learning**

If a £2m business wants to become a £4m business, Narratiive should first ask where the additional £2m can plausibly come from: more buyers, increased frequency, greater conversion, higher pricing, new distribution, new geography, new products or some combination of these.

Marketing strategy then becomes a set of interventions against a defined commercial problem rather than a collection of brand or communications recommendations.

Over time, Narratiive should build an evidence base of client outcomes and use that evidence to strengthen the claims it can responsibly make.

## Market-readiness test for Tony

Tony does not need to be theoretically “finished” before Narratiive goes to market. Tony is market-ready when a real prospect can move coherently through:

**Lead → research → discovery preparation → Blueprint production → creative development → review → client delivery → follow-up**

without Matt becoming the system administrator.

The next stage of Tony development should therefore be driven by live prospects and clients. Put real opportunities through the system, identify what breaks, and fix the failure points. Real client journeys become the development environment.

## Evidence engine: twice-weekly Growth Teardowns

Narratiive needs visible evidence of strategic competence. Publish **twice each week**.

**Tuesday — Growth Teardown**  
Take a real company and identify what appears to be holding growth back.

**Thursday — Growth Opportunity**  
Take a company or category, identify an underexploited opportunity and show how Narratiive would attack it.

The strongest pieces should connect diagnosis to execution:

**What they are doing → what the evidence suggests → Narratiive’s strategic answer → what that answer could look like as creative.**

The objective is not generic marketing education. It is to create a growing body of proof that Narratiive understands businesses, growth and marketing effectiveness.

## Conversation before scale

Narratiive does not currently need thousands of leads. It needs high-quality conversations with founders, Managing Directors and growth leaders in startups, SMBs and scale-ups.

Priority environments include founder networks, accelerators, coworking communities, investor portfolios, startup programmes, local business networks, founder breakfasts, university enterprise communities and referral partners.

The initial invitation can be exploratory rather than sales-led: Narratiive is looking for a small number of ambitious businesses with real growth problems on which to prove and refine the Growth Sprint.

## Consulting while employed

During the validation period, Narratiive should operate as a deliberately small consulting practice rather than attempting to behave like a full agency.

Subject to employment-contract, confidentiality and conflict-of-interest obligations, the initial operating constraint should be approximately **one new Growth Sprint per month**, expanding only as the delivery system and demand prove themselves.

The aim is not to replace employment income immediately. The aim is to prove repeatedly that unrelated businesses will pay Narratiive to solve this problem and that the work can be delivered to a high standard alongside existing commitments.

A future model can combine project-based Growth Sprints with a limited number of ongoing **Narratiive Growth Partner** relationships for strategic direction and campaign development.

## The 90-day market validation objective

By late November 2026, target:

| Measure | Target |
| --- | ---: |
| Growth teardowns published | 24 |
| Proper founder conversations | 30 |
| Growth Sprints sold | 5 |
| Strong case studies | 3 |
| Ongoing Growth Partners | 2 |
| Tony-run live client journeys | 5 |

These are more important validation measures than follower counts, website traffic or newsletter subscriber growth during this phase.

## Product development and customer development become one flywheel

From this point, Narratiive should stop treating “build the product” and “find customers” as separate workstreams.

**Every prospect improves Tony.  
Every conversation improves the proposition.  
Every Sprint improves the Blueprint.  
Every Blueprint produces new intellectual property and teardown material.  
Every campaign creates evidence and content.  
Every client result strengthens Narratiive’s proof.  
Every objection improves the sales process.**

The market becomes part of product development.

## The principle to protect

The Blueprint is not necessarily the thing customers initially desire. It is the reason the work works.

Creative is often what makes the opportunity visible and desirable. That does not reduce the value of strategy; it makes strategy easier to understand and buy.

Narratiive should therefore **use creative to sell the value of the Blueprint**, rather than replacing the Blueprint with creative production.

The operating idea is simple:

> **Growth is the promise. Strategy is the architecture. Creative makes the strategy tangible. Tony makes the delivery scalable.**
