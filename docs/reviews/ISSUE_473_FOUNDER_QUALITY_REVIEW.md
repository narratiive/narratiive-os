# Issue #473 founder-quality review of the FIELDWORK Blueprint

Review basis: PR #472 at `bacf335`, its 20-slide PDF/PPTX, model, ledger, QA and approval package. This is a buyer-quality critique, not the independent merge review required by the AI Constitution.

## £5,000 founder verdict before revision

**Decision: would not buy yet.** The original sample proved that Narratiive could control evidence, generate a coherent artefact and stop at a human gate. It did not yet prove £5,000 of commercial judgement.

The first five slides named a £690,000 revenue scenario, a proposition constraint and three sensible moves. Those numbers came from simultaneous traffic, conversion and order-value assumptions, with no ramp, contribution or investment case. The strategy could have applied to many design-led ecommerce brands. The creative territory sounded promising but showed no creative proof. The presentation was clean, consistent and legible, yet it felt like a well-run internal strategy template rather than a founder-specific conviction piece.

## Priority findings

### P0 - must fix before commercial review

1. **Revenue headroom masqueraded as an opportunity.** The base case omitted contribution, implementation investment and ramp. A paying founder needs to know what must be true before the opportunity deserves cash.
2. **The diagnosis lacked a founder decision.** “Proposition clarity” described a problem but did not force a choice between buying reach and proving a mixed-use proposition.
3. **The creative reveal contained no creative proof.** Text, colours and principles did not demonstrate a distinctive future.
4. **The close served internal governance, not the buyer.** The final slide told Matt to approve a checksum. The founder needed a bounded investment decision; the checksum gate belongs in the approval package.

### P1 - fix for a £5,000 proposition

1. The proposed advantage needed a narrower category claim than “modern work at home”.
2. The three moves needed product and customer-system specificity.
3. The evidence register exposed repository paths instead of explaining what would verify each claim.
4. The deck used repeated card layouts and small footers, creating a controlled but visually cautious rhythm.
5. “Needs validation” placeholders signalled incompleteness without showing a purposeful evidence plan.

### P2 - improve after the commercial review

1. Add verified competitor and customer evidence in a real pilot before changing the positioning map.
2. Test the deck in PowerPoint and Google Slides after a human-approved production template exists.
3. Build a second creative route only when real founder and customer evidence can discriminate between territories.
4. Decide whether the seven-chapter profile becomes canon; PR #470 remains proposed.

## Post-revision founder view

The revised sample is credible enough for **commercial review**, not sale or release. It now demonstrates the product's intended behaviour: a sharp choice, a quantified but challengeable case, a distinctive creative possibility and a bounded next investment. It still relies entirely on fictional assumptions. A real £5,000 purchase requires verified company economics, customer evidence, competitive evidence and Matt's approval of both the offer and the exact client artefact.
