SKETCHBOOK — CANONICAL MASTER CONTRACT v2.0
19 October 2026 · Reusable document and population standard

Purpose
SKETCHBOOK makes the approved strategy visible as a distinctive creative world. A founder should recognise the audience, understand the idea, see how it behaves across channels and know what they are being asked to approve.

FIELDWORK establishes the commercial growth case. SKETCHBOOK expresses the selected strategy creatively. Finished asset production and activation follow separate scopes and approvals. Each product has its own document; a shared client identity connects them.

Source of truth
This contract and the versioned machine specification define the reusable standard. The preserved Rave prototype demonstrates the original five page types; it is an example, not a universal client identity or an approved campaign. The previous specification remains historical.

Fixed architecture, client-specific expression
Use five canonical page types in the order below. These are five types, not a compulsory five-page limit. Repeat territory and application pages to cover the agreed scope. Repeat audience or visual-reference pages when needed; do not squeeze more content into a fixed frame.
The canvas is 1280 × 720, with 58-pixel safe margins. Keep the inspected prototype geometry and type hierarchy. The corrected 12-column grid uses 20-pixel gutters and 78⅔-pixel columns within the safe width.
Every commission supplies a versioned client manifest: identity, palette, typography, imagery direction, product rules and asset rights/provenance. Rave yellow, coffee imagery, domestic kitchens and its anti-reference list belong only to Rave’s manifest. They are never universal defaults.

1. CREATIVE PROPOSITION
Job: establish the central creative idea and why it matters commercially.
Composition: use the existing full-canvas hero with a left content panel; headline at x58/y104, payoff at x58/y532. The inspected template provides the exact rectangles and sizes.
Content: one proposition across two short lines, one emotional payoff, a commercial role and a reference to the approved strategy. The payoff carries the commercial role within the same text allowance; provenance stays in notes.
Image: a relevant hero is required. It must demonstrate the proposition. Record focal point, source, rights and any generated-illustration status.
Fit: retain the existing 18-character limit for each proposition line and 125-character payoff limit. Select concise copy rather than changing the layout. Longer ideas need editorial revision.
Acceptance: the idea and its intended audience response are understandable without narration.

2. AUDIENCE PROFILE
Job: make the person and the buying moment immediately recognisable.
Composition: standard portrait-right layout; portrait x710/y0/w570/h720; copy panel and profile rows retain the existing geometry.
Content: audience name, human truth, motivation, hesitation, buying trigger and the creative response needed. Keep the creative job in the supporting notes where it will not fit the established on-page fields.
Fit: name up to 28 characters; human truth up to 70; each profile row up to 110. The machine record preserves evidence status separately.
Image: a contextually appropriate portrait, never an implied customer testimonial. Generated people are creative illustrations. A real quote requires an identifiable authorised source.
Acceptance: the creative team knows whom to influence and what response to create. Qualify material audience uncertainty proportionately; do not turn the portrait into a warning panel.

3. VISUAL WORLD AND ART DIRECTION
Job: show the visual choices, not just describe an aesthetic.
Composition: existing hero image at x0/y0/w1280/h460; lower rules band begins at y460. Use the defined three rule columns.
Content: one art-direction statement; setting and lighting; composition/casting/product treatment; palette, typography and exclusions. Repeat the same page type for additional reference images if the scope needs them.
Fit: statement up to 38 characters; each rule up to 90; exclusions up to six. Keep the narrative readable at the existing size.
Image: source or generate for the client’s actual world. No automatic generic stock substitution. Product appearance and claims need the appropriate review.
Acceptance: another creative team can reproduce the intended world and recognise what does not belong in it.

4. CAMPAIGN TERRITORY
Job: demonstrate a repeatable creative idea, linked to an audience behaviour and commercial objective.
Composition: title x58/y92/w740/h144; hero execution x58/y286/w742/h294; mechanism at x858/y286/w326/h331.
Content: territory name, its role, hero copy, a repeatable construction, commercial objective and source status. The objective sits within the territory-role allowance; keep detailed reasoning in notes.
Fit: name up to 32 characters; role up to 110; hero copy up to 65. Up to three mechanism steps.
Execution: copy-led work may remain native text; photographic concepts need a relevant visual. A heading with a future-image description is not a finished territory page.
Acceptance: the client can see an idea in action, and the team can create further executions from the same construction.

5. CAMPAIGN EXECUTIONS AND APPLICATIONS
Job: show that the chosen idea becomes a coherent body of communication.
Composition: three existing application slots at x58/y276/w300/h300, x390/y276/w300/h300 and x722/y276/w500/h300.
Content: three scoped applications with channel, purpose, copy, asset source and concept/production status. A fourth application requires a repeated page.
Fit: heading up to 40 characters; introductory line up to 125; execution heading up to 70; concise qualification up to 90.
Image: fit mockups, screens and text-bearing creative completely inside their slots. Do not crop out meaning or stretch content.
Acceptance: applications are adapted to their channels while remaining recognisably one campaign. A mockup is not proof of a working landing page, sent email, published ad or achieved result.

Population contract
Inputs: exact approved FIELDWORK argument, the selected Campaign World version, scoped territories and channels, client manifest, evidence register and rights-cleared asset manifest.
Claude develops the creative within that strategy. Tony checks completeness, routes revisions and preserves lineage. The renderer populates fixed slots; it does not invent strategy or redesign the pages.
Map additional v2 metadata to notes or existing allowances, as specified above. No new primary text boxes are authorised by adding a field.
Return missing content to its owner. Internal drafts may show a clear missing-content marker; incomplete pages must not be released as finished work.
If text does not fit, shorten or choose alternate copy through editorial review. Never clip, silently shrink below the established minimum or omit a material qualification. If it still fails, block the page.
Only the inspected standard composition is defined for each type. Alternative layouts listed in the old prototype were not fully specified; they require their own versioned geometry and rendered review before use.

Evidence and review
Keep source IDs, prompts, generation receipts, detailed assumptions and technical production instructions in notes and the internal handoff.
Visible qualifications must remain sufficient to prevent misleading claims. Distinguish concept work from finished assets. Never invent quotes, current packaging, performance, service promises or live UI behaviour.
Check native editability, crop, text fit, readable labels, visual relevance and continuity across every rendered page. Structural tests alone do not establish visual quality.
Matt approves the exact version, territories and scope for client presentation or production. Finalising this template does not approve a Rave or KatKin campaign.

Production handoff
An approved SKETCHBOOK supplies the chosen territories, client visual rules, approved copy/claim set, application requirements and asset references to the Creative Director’s Bible and Production Pack. Those remain the internal canonical objects.
The customer-facing noun for finished creative production is not settled in this contract. Preserve internal object names until the naming decision is recovered and confirmed.
Production readiness separately requires exact formats, rights, budget, product accuracy, QA and approval. Activation requires its own platform access, launch and spend authority.

Version and operational state
v2.0 separates reusable layout from client art direction and clarifies repetition, population, missing content and production handoff. It preserves the original Rave prototype and source history.
The document and machine specification are prepared for implementation. The current Rave builder is a prototype with fixed example copy and automatic fit settings; it is not a verified generic population renderer. Runtime adoption must use the v2 inputs and demonstrate a second client without Rave leakage before being labelled production-ready.
No new visual page variant has been produced or visually validated in this revision.
