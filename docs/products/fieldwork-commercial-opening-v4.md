FIELDWORK — CANONICAL MASTER TEMPLATE v4.0
Opening contract · 19 October 2026
Owner: Matt · Scope: reusable FIELDWORK structure and editorial standard.

Purpose
FIELDWORK gives a founder a compelling, evidence-led view of where the business can go and how to get there. The opening makes the commercial opportunity tangible before the detailed diagnosis. It respects the business already built and makes a clear recommendation.

Standing requirement
Every FIELDWORK starts with the five content slides below, in this order. An optional cover precedes them. Detailed market research, diagnosis, strategy, campaign direction, implementation and measurement follow and substantiate the opening. A five-slide export uses these same five slides; it must not generate a competing summary. Preserve any agreed total deck length by consolidating repeated material.

This is the master template for future work, not a request to revise the preserved Rave benchmark. Examples are patterns to populate from each business's evidence, never reusable client claims. The historical schema retained below is supporting material; where its opening order or editorial framing conflicts, this v4.0 opening governs the revised document.

SLIDE 1 — THE COMMERCIAL POSITION TODAY
Question: What has this business built, how does it make money, and what is the next growth opportunity?
Required content: the business model; its strongest relevant assets; the commercial baseline and timeframe; one clear growth question. Use actual revenue, customers, conversion, repeat, order value or margin where supplied and relevant. Describe an absent measure as an input to obtain, not a business failure.
Headline pattern: “[Existing strength] gives [business] a platform to [specific commercial opportunity].”
Commercial takeaway: identify where the next increment of customers, revenue or contribution could come from.
Visual: one strong NOW composition, showing two or three relevant baseline measures or business assets, with one commercial question. Use an explicit data-to-confirm label when necessary. No fabricated baseline or diagnosis.

SLIDE 2 — THE 12-MONTH OPPORTUNITY
Question: What could become materially better over the next year, and what would that mean commercially?
Required content: a desirable future state; the gains sought; a transparent relationship between those gains and revenue or contribution; the assumptions needed to estimate scale.
Headline pattern: “In 12 months, [business] could [specific valuable change] by [commercial mechanism].”
Show the prize prominently. For a repeat-purchase business this might mean more qualified buyers, more of them making a first purchase, and more valuable repeat relationships. Adapt the mechanism for services, wholesale or other business models.
With first-party inputs, show a baseline and credible scenarios, including a chosen planning case and sensitivity. Without those inputs, use a clearly labelled illustrative indexed scenario or a meaningful qualitative ambition with the exact inputs needed to quantify it. Missing data must not collapse the page into a warning or an empty equation.
Visual: NOW → 12 MONTHS, with a dominant gain and its supporting calculation. Put the concise scenario label beside the figure; detailed sources and assumptions belong in notes or the evidence register.

SLIDE 3 — WHERE THE GAINS COME FROM
Question: Which two to four changes make that opportunity achievable?
For each lever, specify the action, the behaviour it should change, the commercial consequence and the measure that will show progress.
Headline pattern: “[Specific changes] can turn [existing strength] into [commercial gain].”
Examples of mechanisms: distinctive creative reaches more qualified buyers; clearer choice improves first-purchase conversion; useful replenishment increases repeat orders; a better product mix increases contribution. These are mechanisms to evaluate, not universal recommendations.
Connect the levers as one argument. Explain what can run together and what genuinely depends on another change. Do not impose “fix everything before growth” or an arbitrary media budget.
Visual: an editable bridge from the current baseline to the opportunity, with each lever's contribution or role clearly visible.

SLIDE 4 — THE AUDIENCE OPPORTUNITY
Question: Whose behaviour can create these gains, and why is this business well placed to win?
Required content: a specific buying situation, the people experiencing it, what they value, the barrier or trigger, why the offer fits, and the commercial role of winning them.
Headline pattern: “Win [specific people] when [buying moment] by [credible reason to choose].”
Use customer evidence where available. Distinguish observed segments from audience hypotheses. A hypothesis can be stated with conviction as a proposed priority, with its validation method recorded alongside the evidence.
Visual: a recognisable customer moment, supported by a real quote or sourced signal and a clear route to purchase or repeat. Avoid invented personas and demographic filler.

SLIDE 5 — THE STRATEGIC DIRECTION
Question: What should the business choose, and what becomes possible if it does?
Required content: one memorable strategic direction, why it follows from the preceding four slides, the first concrete action, and the success measure.
Headline pattern: “[Specific strategic direction].”
Supporting line: “By [choice], [business] can [commercial gain], starting with [first action].”
End on the opportunity and the founder's decision. Record material trade-offs and operational dependencies in the supporting detail; surface any that could change the decision on this slide. Do not invent a harmful diagnosis or use a generic “prove the path” instruction as a substitute for a strategic answer.
Visual: one confident statement and a credible view of the future business or brand, followed by the first action.

COMMERCIAL MODELLING STANDARD
Use a model appropriate to the business, with an explicit period and population.
For a new-customer cohort: qualified visits × first-purchase conversion × realised revenue per acquired customer in the stated period. The final term may be decomposed into order frequency × average order value.
Treat existing-customer revenue separately; add wholesale or other channels only when populations do not overlap. Retention and order frequency must not count the same repeat behaviour twice.
Show baseline versus scenario and the incremental difference. Revenue is not profit: contribution requires the relevant product, fulfilment, service and acquisition costs. When those costs are unavailable, label the output as revenue.
Indexed arithmetic example only: 10% more qualified visits × 10% higher conversion × 10% more revenue per acquired customer = 1.331, or 33.1% more revenue for that modelled cohort. This is a sensitivity demonstration, not a target, forecast, benchmark or claim about any client, and it does not imply a 33.1% increase in total company revenue.
Do not assign invented probabilities, fixed budgets, market shares, client economics or guarantees. Link scenario choices to evidence, feasible actions, capacity and timing. Keep material assumptions visible; do not imply a fully achieved improvement from day one.

EDITORIAL AND DESIGN STANDARD
Lead with the opportunity and earn it through evidence. Be ambitious, commercially precise and plain-spoken. Each slide contributes a new step to one cumulative argument.
Avoid unearned statements that the client is broken, behind, leaking value or wasting money. State a supported material problem clearly and constructively when it changes the recommendation. Do not hide adverse evidence or remove a material dependency to make a scenario look better.
Use one dominant takeaway per slide, short supporting copy and generous space. Make the commercial gain at least as visually prominent as the diagnosis. Apply a coherent client-specific art direction across FIELDWORK and later deliverables.
Headlines should communicate the business conclusion, not the internal process. Evidence labels, provenance, model assumptions and caveats stay legible and proportionate. They must not overwhelm the argument or disappear from the work.
Do not let a later schema, prompt, summariser or renderer replace the approved five-slide argument with database fields, generic copy or a different strategy.

ACCEPTANCE AND VERSION CONTROL
The reviewer must be able to answer, from the opening alone: where is the business now; what could it gain; how; with whom; and what should it do first?
Check that the gains follow from the model, every factual claim has a source, scenarios are labelled, each slide adds something new, and the recommendation is specific enough to guide a decision.
Matt's instruction approves revising this template structure. It does not approve an existing or future client deliverable, a production deployment, a live campaign or a financial commitment. Keep the existing human approval gates.
Record template version, source evidence, scenario inputs, exact reviewed artefact and revisions in every future handoff. Preserve previous template versions and benchmarks. Automated production is not updated merely because this document has changed.
